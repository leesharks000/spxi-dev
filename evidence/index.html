EvidenceWhat each kind of evidence measures

Semantic Economy Institute · computed from the Capture Registry v12.43 · 2026-10-02 · CC BY 4.0

Evidence is weighed by the attribute it measures.

A strong design on one attribute is no evidence about another.

Three bodies of evidence are offered about generative search, and each measures a different attribute. The classical GEO literature measures citation of content already placed in a model's context; AEO reporting measures answer selection and referral traffic; the SPXI Capture Registry measures whether a named entity is composed as itself at named addresses, on independent surfaces, over time and after its source is removed. Evidence is weighed by whether it measures the attribute a claim concerns.

Three kinds of evidence

Classical GEO

Source
Aggarwal et al., GEO: Generative Engine Optimization (KDD 2024), and the experimental literature after it

Design
Controlled experiment. Source content is rewritten and placed in a generative engine’s context; the generated answer is scored.

What it measures
How much of a generated answer is drawn from, or cites, content that is already in the context.

What it does not measure
Whether content is discovered, whether an entity resolves as itself, whether any effect lasts. The 2026 critical survey of 45 studies cited on this site’s front page states that the literature establishes neither organic discoverability nor durable traffic.

AEO practice

Source
Practitioner and agency reporting on client properties

Design
Mostly before-and-after reporting on the reporter’s own properties.

What it measures
Whether an answer engine selects a page or names an entity for a query, and the referral traffic that follows.

What it does not measure
Whether the answer is reached by judging the entity or by being told (see SPXI ≠ AEO); durability.

SPXI Capture Registry

Source
EA-WG-CAPTURES-01, v12.43: 516 addresses, 690 observations

Design
Longitudinal observation, first-party maintained. The compositions are made by independently operated systems; the Institute records them.

What it measures
Whether a named entity is composed as itself at a named address, on independent surfaces, over time, including after the originating source stops resolving.

What it does not measure
Causal efficacy against matched untreated entities; effect size over GEO or conventional structured data; which component does what; return on investment.

Why they are weighed by attribute

A controlled design that measures citation share supports a claim about citation share. It says nothing about whether an entity resolves as itself or whether that lasts, because it does not measure either. An observational record of entity resolution over time supports a claim about entity resolution over time, and on its own establishes nothing about what caused it. A reader who ranks these by the shape of the study, before asking whether the study measures the attribute in question, compares answers to different questions.

SPXI’s claim is entity inscription: that an entity constructed under the protocol is composed as itself by systems that retrieve it, on independent surfaces, and goes on being composed after its originating source is gone. The evidence that bears on that claim is evidence of entity composition at named addresses over time. The Capture Registry is that evidence. Whether SPXI causes it, and by how much against matched untreated entities, is the open question Phase I states before it runs.

What the registry does not establish

  • That SPXI caused any given composition. Causal efficacy against matched untreated entities is open (Phase I).
  • An effect size over classical GEO or conventional structured data. That comparison has not been run.
  • Which component of the protocol produces which effect.
  • A return on investment. SPXI makes no ROI claim; an earlier table was withdrawn in full, with its sources traced, on the front page.

An open causal question does not remove an observed event, and an observed event does not by itself establish its cause.
